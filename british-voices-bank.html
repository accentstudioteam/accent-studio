The British Voices Bank

The British
Voices Bank

Open voice data for the languages the world forgot.

A UK-national archive of consensually sourced, linguistically verified voice data for under-represented languages. Free to researchers, educators, public bodies, and small developers. Built and maintained in Britain by Accent AI Studio.

The gap

The world has more than 7,000 languages. AI speaks fewer than 100 of them.

Over 2,000 languages are spoken across the African continent. More than 1.3 billion people speak them. Fewer than 20 are meaningfully represented in the voice datasets that train modern AI.

The same gap exists across the Commonwealth, across the Global South, and across heritage communities everywhere. When a language cannot be heard by the machines that increasingly mediate our daily lives, it slips a little further out of the modern world.

This is not just a technology problem. It is a cultural, educational, economic, and civic problem. It is also solvable.

How it works

A national asset, built from a company's commitment.

The British Voices Bank is funded by Accent AI Studio Ltd through a long-term commitment to deposit at least 20% of its annual commercial production volume into the Bank, at no cost to users. Every hour of commercial voice work we produce for AI labs generates a deposit of verified, consented voice data for the public archive.

01 · Consent-first

Consent-first contribution.

Every voice contributor consents explicitly to how their data will be used, where it will go, and how long it will be retained. We never scrape, never collect without consent, and never anonymise identity against a contributor's wishes.

02 · Verified

Linguistically verified.

Every batch is reviewed by trained linguists before archival. Transcriptions, annotations, dialect tags, and metadata are checked for accuracy. The quality standard for Bank data is the same as our commercial product.

03 · Deposited

Deposited into the Bank.

A minimum of 20% of our annual commercial production volume is deposited into the British Voices Bank each year. Deposits are made in batches, with full documentation, by linguistic family and dialect.

04 · Open

Openly licensed, freely usable.

Bank data is released under an open licence (CC BY 4.0 target), with attribution to contributors and the Bank. Available via API, bulk download, and curated collections, at no cost to approved users.

The archive

What's inside, and what's coming.

Q2 2027 · Launch

At initial public release in Q2 2027, the Bank will contain approximately 1,000 hours of verified voice data across 10 African languages, including Nigerian Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Swahili, Amharic, Zulu, Xhosa, Twi, and Wolof. Each language will include multiple dialects, multiple genders, a range of ages, and a variety of regional accents.

End of 2027 · Expansion

By end 2027, we are targeting 10,000 hours across 25 African languages, with first deposits of Commonwealth languages (Caribbean creoles, Punjabi, Bengali, Tamil) beginning Q4 2027.

2030 · Vision

By 2030, we are targeting 100,000+ hours across 100+ under-represented languages worldwide, making the British Voices Bank the world's largest consensually sourced, linguistically verified open voice archive.

Who it's for

Free, under an open licence, for the people and projects that need it most.

UK Government and public bodies.

Available to all UK Government departments, public bodies, and local authorities for public-interest AI applications, educational tooling, and digital-service accessibility.

Academic researchers.

Open to UK and international universities for linguistics, AI, phonology, dialectology, and model-fairness research. Attribution requested, use freely.

Educators and schools.

Available for use in language-learning tools, heritage language teaching, Saturday schools, and overseas educational partnerships. No fees.

Independent and small AI developers.

Free use for AI developers building inclusive products, with reasonable scale thresholds. Larger-scale commercial use is handled separately by Accent AI Studio under its commercial licence.

Trust

Built on consent, verified by linguists, held in trust.

Consent-first.

No voice data enters the Bank without the contributor's explicit, informed consent for public release. Contributors can withdraw their consent at any time, and we honour withdrawal within 30 days of request. We publish a plain-English consent policy and are governed by UK and international data protection law.

Linguistically verified.

We work with trained linguists for every language in the Bank. Every recording is verified for accuracy of transcription, annotation, and language tagging before archival. We publish verification standards and audit reports annually.

Open by default, protected where needed.

All Bank data is released under an open licence. In specific cases (sensitive content, contributor requests, cultural sensitivity), access may be gated to approved researchers with ethics committee review. Access policies are published and reviewed with partners.

We are preparing a formal governance board of UK and international linguists, data ethicists, Commonwealth cultural representatives, and public-interest technologists, to be convened before the Bank's public release.

Contribute

Lend your voice, keep your language alive.

Get paid to help build the British Voices Bank. Every voice makes the Bank bigger. Every language gets a chance.

What it means to contribute.

You record short pieces of speech, conversation, and language improv, guided by prompts, in your native language. Every recording is reviewed by a linguist. Every contribution gets you paid. Every voice gets credited.

What you earn.

We pay competitively for every verified minute of voice contributed. Payment rates vary by language and demand, but transparency is a core principle: our rates are published publicly on our contributor dashboard.

What happens to your voice.

Your voice data is used in two ways: in Accent Studio's commercial dataset for AI labs, and in the British Voices Bank for public use. You consent to both uses explicitly before you begin. You can withdraw at any time.

Who can contribute.

Native speakers of any under-represented language. Starting with African languages. Expanding to Commonwealth and global under-represented languages over 2027 and beyond.

How to get started.

Sign up at the Accent Studio contributor platform. Complete your profile and language verification. Begin contributing from your phone or computer, at your own pace.

Licence and governance

Open by default. Governed in the public interest.

The licence (target).

British Voices Bank data will be released under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0), subject to final confirmation at launch. This means you can use Bank data freely for research, education, and non-commercial AI development, provided you credit the Bank and its contributors.

Attribution.

When using Bank data, cite the British Voices Bank (britishvoicesbank.org), include a reference to the specific dataset version, and credit individual contributors where they have requested named attribution.

Commercial use.

For large-scale commercial use of Bank data, please contact Accent AI Studio's commercial team at hello@accentstudio.io. Commercial data licences are negotiated separately and support the Bank's sustainability.

Governance.

The British Voices Bank is maintained by Accent AI Studio Ltd. Operational decisions on the Bank's content, licensing, and release are reviewed by an external governance board (in formation), composed of UK and international linguists, data ethicists, Commonwealth cultural representatives, and public-interest technologists.

Consent framework.

Full consent policy will be published at launch. Core principles: contributors give informed, specific, revocable consent before any voice data enters the Bank. Consent can be withdrawn at any time by contacting contributor support. We honour withdrawal within 30 days.

Compliance.

The Bank is operated in compliance with UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018, and relevant international data protection frameworks. Full compliance statement will be published at launch.
